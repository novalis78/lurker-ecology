## Abstract (plain text, 1049 characters; arXiv limit 1920)

If technological civilizations are common, which resolution of the Fermi paradox survives? We introduce defector robustness: an explanation of the silence must survive a few deviating civilizations. Every silence explanation either lacks enforcement and fails, or adds local enforcement and becomes a lurker hypothesis; zoo, aestivation and dark forest differ only in trigger settings. Because the suppression window is short compared with light-lag, and fast strikes are visible, the timely and quiet enforcer is a self-replicating probe resident in the target system. Rival lineages turn suppression into a volunteer's dilemma whose no-strike probability rises with crowding; survivors that reason as we do seed lineages of their own, so those arising under a lone established lineage fare worst. Earth's survival admits four branches. Under lethal enforcement all predict an excess of impact scars among technosignature planets, including fluorinated ghost atmospheres, over matched controls; the biological premise predicts common oxygen worlds.

## Abstract, option B: same claims, reflowed for readability (1277 characters)

If technological civilizations are common, which explanation of the Fermi paradox survives? We propose a test we call defector robustness: an explanation of the silence must still work when a few civilizations refuse to follow it. Every explanation fails this test unless it includes local enforcement, and once it does, it becomes a hypothesis about resident probes. The zoo, aestivation and dark-forest scenarios then differ only in what triggers the probes. The window in which a young civilization can be suppressed is short compared with interstellar light-lag, and fast strikes are visible, so the enforcer that is both timely and quiet is a self-replicating probe already present in the target system. When several rival lineages of such probes share a system, suppression becomes a volunteer's dilemma, and the probability that no one strikes rises with crowding. Survivors that reason as we do seed lineages of their own, so a civilization arising under a single established lineage fares worst. Earth's survival admits four branches. Under lethal enforcement, all four predict an excess of impact scars among technosignature planets, including fluorinated ghost atmospheres, relative to matched controls; the biological premise predicts that oxygen worlds are common.
